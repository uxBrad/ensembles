# Echo Ensembles

These are worked-example ensembles used to demonstrate the framework. They model realistic configurations relevant to Echo, an accessibility audit software product.

## What Echo is

Echo is a software product (in development) for accessibility auditors. It serves multiple customer types:

- Centralized accessibility teams (Hartwell-shaped)
- Solo accessibility consultants (forthcoming)
- Embedded auditors on product teams (forthcoming)

Echo's value depends not just on individual auditor workflow, but on how findings travel from auditors into receiving product teams — which is why the framework models *both* sides of that interaction.

## Available ensembles

### `hartwell-group.md`
A four-person centralized accessibility team inside a financial services company. Mature, technically strong, politically tired. Represents one of Echo's primary customer archetypes.

### `team-meridian.md`
A six-person Scrum team owning customer onboarding at a SaaS company. Receives accessibility findings from teams like Hartwell. Represents the *receiving* side of the engagement — not an Echo customer themselves, but the context where Echo's outputs land.

## What's not yet here

- A solo consultant ensemble (team-of-one)
- An immature accessibility team variant
- An embedded auditor on a product team
- The engagement pattern document for Hartwell × Meridian

These are planned. The two existing ensembles are sufficient to demonstrate the schema and methodology.

## Important caveat

These ensembles are **hypothetical**, built to illustrate the framework rather than from primary research. In real practice, ensembles should be grounded in research, with per-field evidence citations. These examples don't include such citations because there's no underlying research to cite.

When you build your own ensembles, they should be more rigorously grounded than these examples are. The examples show the *structure* and *level of detail*, not the citation discipline.
